Editing the review tables in place

Today the three sheets at the top of a cycle report are read-only: a wrong salary or a missing contribution means going back to Excel and re-uploading. This proposal makes those three tables editable in place — change a cell, add a row, remove one — and one Recalculate button saves the change and regenerates every table below it. Uploading a sheet still works exactly as it does now, for the first load.

How it behaves

Recalculate is the save

There is no separate Save. One button writes the three sheets and re-runs the engine, so the tables below can never show figures the database doesn’t hold.

It stays greyed out until something actually changes, and the gold Unsaved edits chip is on screen the whole time your edits are only in the browser — it disappears the moment they land.

Same rules as an upload

A name and a client are required and can’t repeat; salary, revenue and cost must be numbers; type is PRJ or RET. A cell that fails says so in plain words and nothing is saved until it’s fixed.

A client short of 100% still saves

That is a payout rule, not a save rule — it already blocks that client from the payout and flags it in red. You can save half-finished shares and come back; you just won’t get paid figures for that client until they add up.

Status is chosen, not guessed

On upload the status is worked out from the closure-date column. In the editor it’s a plain dropdown — Ongoing, Ended, In progress, Pending — because you’re looking straight at it. Setting a closure date does not silently change it.

Discard means discard

Leaving edit mode with unsaved edits asks first. Discard puts every cell back to what the database holds — nothing half-applied.

Uploading still works

The upload row is untouched, and a re-upload still replaces that whole sheet. Editing is for the correction you spotted on screen; the upload is for the first load.

One thing to decide

If you rename someone in the People table — say a typo in “Nour Hassan” — should we rename them everywhere else at the same time (as Lead, as BD, as a contributions column)? Saying yes keeps the cycle intact from one edit. Saying no means the name only changes where you changed it, and the report will flag the lead as “not in the people sheet” until you fix the others by hand.
