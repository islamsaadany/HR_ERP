2 The same announcement, two people

One message, written once. Each person's copy carries their own unit — which means the send is one message per person, not one mail to a list. SMP already solved that: Resend's batch endpoint takes up to 100 separate messages in a single request, so nobody is in anybody else's To line and a failure names who.

4 What we take from SMP already proven there

Six things, each of which SMP paid for once already.

Email is not the web

Tables not divs (Outlook renders through Word — no flexbox, no grid). Every style inline (Gmail strips <style> on some clients). Colours written literally — var() is not supported.

The preview IS the builder

One function makes the HTML; the on-screen preview and the real send call it. A preview drawn any other way is a picture of an email nobody receives.

Send yourself one first

A test send on the setup page, showing the real design, the real sender name and the real address — before anybody else gets one.

Ask Resend if the domain is verified

Until it is, Resend delivers only to the account owner — so a test that “worked” fails for everyone else, silently. The page should say ready or ready, for you only.

One message per person, one request

Batch of up to 100 separate messages. Never a shared To, never BCC: nobody sees anybody's address, and a failure says which person. It is also what lets each copy carry its own unit.

The address and the name live apart

Address in the environment (tied to the verified domain — a deployment decision). Display name in settings (changeable on a Tuesday). Already how this app works.

5 Two things I measured findings

Both are in the emails this platform already sends today.

The gold eyebrow on the navy bar is below AA

Eyebrow colour on navy-800RatioVerdict
gold-600 #a8821e — what we send today4.33:1fails small text
gold-500 #c9a2276.40:1passes
gold-300 #e0c05a8.74:1passes

AA wants 4.5:1 for text under 18px; our eyebrow is 12px. This is exactly the trap SMP records walking into five times — which is why in the design above the accent is a band, not type: a fill has no ratio to meet.

The unit logos cannot go in the email as things stand

Unit logos are stored as private blobs and streamed through the server behind a sign-in check. A mail client fetching that URL is not signed in, so it gets a broken-image box. (Data-URI images do not rescue it — Gmail and Outlook block those outright.) Two ways out: stay typographic, as drawn above, or serve unit logos from a public URL — which is a decision about making a logo public, and yours rather than mine.

What I’m assuming — correct me and I’ll change it

  • “Forefront Group” is written out in the email, not “FFG”. On the sender name you said People of FFG — I’d suggest People of Forefront Group for the same reason, since an inbox often truncates the sender line and an abbreviation is the worst thing to be left with. Your call; it is one setting either way.
  • The group is a real business unit named Forefront Group, marked as the default — not a new layer above the units. Everything an email needs is already on the unit record.
  • One template, brand as data. Not a template per unit — that is how two brands drift apart.
  • The unit never touches the body or the button, only the kicker and the band. Anything else means a unit choosing a pale colour makes its own email unreadable.
  • Congrats goes to the person only, gift included, drafted and sent by a human.
  • The from-name change affects the two existing workflows too — a benefit-claim email would also arrive from People of Forefront Group. I think that is right (one voice), but it is a visible change to something already live.
