What this changes, and what it deliberately doesn't

Only the phone is affected. Nothing on a desktop screen moves.

Added A menu button in the phone header

Tapping it slides the section list over the page. Tapping a section, the ✕, the dimmed page, or pressing Escape closes it. It also closes on its own when you land on the new page, so you are never left with the menu covering what you just chose.

Added A dot on the button when something is waiting

Time-Off decisions, courses to finish, confirmations, congratulations to send and requested profile fields all already produce a badge on desktop. On a phone they are invisible behind a closed menu, so the button carries a single gold dot when any of them is above zero.

Added Room for the notch and the home bar

Installed and run full-screen, the navy header currently sits under the phone's clock and the page runs into the home bar. The app will pad itself to the phone's safe area so neither happens.

Added One missing tag for Chrome

We emit Apple's full-screen tag but not the modern one Chrome now reads. Invisible on screen; it removes a warning and keeps installation reliable on Android.

Moved Sign out

Today it is the only thing in the phone header. It moves into the menu, next to your name and the account switcher — the same place it lives on desktop.

Unchanged Every page, every colour, every desktop screen

No page is redesigned, nothing is re-laid-out for small screens, and no section is hidden or reordered. Wide tables still scroll sideways inside their own box exactly as they do now.

Before I build it

Two things worth a word. First, the order: the menu keeps the desktop order, which puts Home, Onboarding and Benefits first. On a phone the sections people actually open are probably Time-Off, Benefits and the Directory — say the word if you want phone order to differ, and I will redraw this before touching anything.

Second, the grouping: your Payments, Petty cash, Confirmations and Admin entries sit under a small "Also yours" heading rather than floating in gold with no label, because on a narrow screen the colour alone reads as decoration. That heading is new — it does not exist on desktop.
