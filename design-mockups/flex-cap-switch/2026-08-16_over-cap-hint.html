Explaining a claim that sits above the cap

Turn the limit off, claim past half, turn it back on: the card then reads "Used 9,000" beside a 6,250 cap. Both numbers are right; together they look like a contradiction. A hover explains it — and only appears when it actually applies.

Bubble shown open here; live it appears on hover or keyboard focus.

  • It only appears when a benefit is genuinely above the cap. On an ordinary cycle the marker isn't rendered at all — no permanent question mark inviting a question nobody has.
  • It names the benefit. "One of your benefits is over" would send the employee hunting; naming it means the sentence is finished.
  • It says the claim stands. The employee's real worry on seeing this is whether money is about to be taken back. That answer comes first, before the consequence.
  • Hover and keyboard focus, with the marker focusable — a mouse-only tooltip hides the explanation from anyone using a keyboard.
  • Nothing else on the card moves: same rows, same numbers, same order. The marker sits inline after the existing label.
