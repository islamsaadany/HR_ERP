The 50% cap, switched per cycle

The per-benefit cap is right over twelve months and perverse over five. HR can switch it off for one short cycle — and the employee is told which rule they are actually under.

  • One row per cycle, under the dates it belongs to — the limit is a property of that cycle, not a platform setting.
  • Only while the cycle is open. A closed cycle shows its setting and no button; the rule its claims were judged under stays readable afterwards.
  • Who turned it off, and when, sits in the row. It is a money rule — the decision needs a name against it.

  • The row stays in place and changes its answer — the employee reads the same line either way, so the difference is legible rather than a missing row.

  • The pool ceiling is untouched. Nothing here suggests the ceiling lifted — only the per-benefit split.

  • Re-enabling never reverses a claim. The claim reimbursed money already spent against a receipt; a rule change afterwards cannot unspend it. A benefit past the line simply has nothing left — zero, never a negative.
  • The last row is why the switch is safe. With the limit off, the pool itself is the most one benefit can hold — so an extension that at least doubles the ceiling always lands the claim back under cap. Extending a cycle is a separate piece of work, not part of this.
