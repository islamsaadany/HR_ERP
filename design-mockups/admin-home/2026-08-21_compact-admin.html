Which one — and the one thing all three cost you

  • A — recommended. Fits one view, keeps the sections, and still looks like the rest of the app. The smallest change to how you already navigate.
  • B is denser and scans well once learned, but it stops looking like a dashboard.
  • C is the smallest and the hardest to learn — with 15 items and no headings, you find things by memory.

The cost, in all three: the attention breakdown tags — “2 claims to review”, “1 over-charged” — no longer sit on the card. They were added deliberately in August so a card says what you are walking into, not just that something is waiting. In these layouts a count stays visible and the breakdown moves into the hover. If that matters more than compactness, A can keep the tags on flagged cards only — those are rarely more than two at a time.
