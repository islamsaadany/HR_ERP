Releasing a cycle’s payments

A finished cycle works out what each person has earned, and there it stops — the figures sit on a report and somebody re-types them somewhere else. This adds the missing step: the business unit head releases the payments they agree to, and those join the queue Finance already works from, ending at the same bank confirmation as everything else the company pays.

The unit’s head New
Releases payments
Only for the unit they head. Nobody else, and no other unit.
Finance
Creates the transaction
In the bank, then submits it for confirmation.
The unit’s confirmer
Confirms at the bank
Or returns it to Finance with a reason.
The person New

Only now has anybody been paid, so only now are they told.

1 One screen for the whole chain

Today’s Who confirms transactions screen widens into Who moves money — the same place in Admin, showing all three steps per business unit, in the order the money moves. Deliberately not called “roles”: that word already means an account’s role, and reusing it here is the confusion we are removing.

Two of the three are appointments; the middle one is an account role. Releasing and confirming are held per business unit, because each unit banks separately. Being Finance is not about one unit, so it stays on the person’s employee record — shown here, but set there, so two screens never claim to own the same setting. Only a Super User can appoint, and no appointment can hand itself out.

5 The message — this is the part to align on

Sent to the person only when the confirmer marks the transaction complete, never when it is released and never when Finance creates it — the same rule the benefit and payback emails already follow: nobody is told money arrived until it has. This is the default wording; the next screen is where you change it.

To ahmed.fathy@forefront.consulting  ·  Subject Your incentive payment has been transferred — EGP 85,130

If anything here doesn’t look right, reply to this message or speak to Finance.
Three choices inside that message, so you can overrule any of them.
  • One email, not two. A person’s fee and commission are separate payments, but if both are confirmed in the same transaction they arrive as one message with two lines — rather than two near-identical emails a second apart.
  • No link. Most people receiving this cannot open the incentive module, so a button would lead them to a locked door. The message stands on its own.
  • No breakdown by client. The amount and what it is for, nothing more. An emailed list of which deals earned what lives in an inbox forever and gets forwarded by accident.

7 Correcting a cycle after some of it has been paid

You can still fix anyone who hasn’t been released. What is refused is an edit that would change a figure already sent — that money has gone, and the report has to keep agreeing with what the bank actually did. The refusal names the person and says which line.

How it behaves

Releasing is not paying

Release puts a payment in Finance’s queue — money the company has agreed it owes. Nothing has moved. The person is paid, and told, when the confirmer marks the transaction complete at the bank.

You release your unit, and only yours

The appointment is per business unit, like the confirmer one. Heading Forefront does not let you release Visual Shift’s people, and no admin account holds it implicitly. A unit with nobody appointed says so plainly rather than sitting there looking empty.

Two lines per person, never merged

Scheme fees and commission are released, sent and confirmed separately, so each traces back to the rule that produced it. You can release one and hold the other.

Nobody is paid twice

A released line leaves the choosing screen and shows its state on the report instead. Releasing the same amount twice isn’t something to remember not to do — it isn’t offered, and the server refuses it even if two people try at once.

The unit is worked out, never chosen

Which account pays comes from the person being paid, exactly as a payback or a benefit claim already works. No dropdown to get wrong, and one release can only hold one unit’s people.

Matched on Employee ID, checked on name

The ID decides who is paid; the name is the cross-check. If the ID lands on nobody, on two people, or on somebody whose name differs from the sheet, the line is held and says which — never guessed at. Guessing who somebody is means guessing whose bank account the money leaves from.

One consequence: the cycle’s People sheet, its template and its review table all gain an Employee ID column.

Finance keeps one queue

Released incentive payments land in the same list Finance already works from, beside paybacks, petty-cash top-ups and benefit reimbursements, grouped by unit exactly as now. There is nothing new for Finance to find or learn.

One thing left, and one thing settled

One person may hold two of the three — settled. You release and confirm for Forefront, and the screen will not warn or nag about it: it is a deliberate arrangement, not a mistake to be flagged every time it is looked at. What the platform still guarantees is that each act is a separate, recorded step with its own moment and its own name against it — and the real control on the money remains the two signatures at the bank. Visual Shift stays split: Alaa releases, Mariam confirms.

Employee ID is optional today, and some people may not have one. Anyone without one cannot be paid through this until HR fills it in. That is the correct behaviour — but it is worth knowing before the first release, rather than discovering it with a cycle open. I can tell you exactly who is missing one as soon as you want to run it.
