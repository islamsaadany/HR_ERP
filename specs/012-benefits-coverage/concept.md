## Part 1 — Our philosophy

Our benefits basket rests on a single belief: **benefits work best when the individual chooses them, the company and the employee share in them, and the greatest support goes to what makes people better.** Five principles follow from that — read from the surface down.

**1\. Yours to choose.** No two people need the same things. The basket is a diverse menu, and each employee builds their own — full-timers selecting up to five benefits (part-timers up to two) that fit their life, their stage, and their priorities. The company sets the frame; the individual makes the choices inside it.

**2\. Rewarded for loyalty.** The longer you stay, the more the company invests in you. Each tenure band unlocks a larger pool — and the increases *grow* with every band, so each additional year of commitment is worth more than the last. Loyalty is treated as accumulated value, not simply time served.

**3\. A shared investment.** The company does not simply hand money over. It shares in each benefit alongside the employee. That shared contribution is deliberate: when we invest in something together, we both commit to it, and the benefit is used with intent rather than taken for granted. The employee is a partner in their benefits, not a passive recipient.

**4\. We invest most in what makes you better.** Not all benefits do the same thing for a person — and our coverage reflects that. The more a benefit develops the employee, the more the company funds it: wellbeing essentials fully, developmental benefits largely, lifestyle and convenience the least. Coverage is a statement of what we believe is worth investing in — which is why wellbeing carries no barrier, and why the company deliberately supports lifestyle least, so people are drawn toward what grows them. *(The coverage tiers are set out in Part 4.)*

**5\. A clear, bounded commitment.** The scheme is honest on both sides. The pool sets the ceiling on what the company contributes; the employee funds anything beyond it. Benefits are reimbursed against real, evidenced spend and claimed within the year — with no carry-over and no cash alternative. A generous frame, clearly bounded.

## Part 2 — How it works

Every eligible employee has two layers of benefits:

- **The guaranteed core** — entitlements everyone receives automatically, with no choice involved. Life-event gifts, allowances, development funding, and the loan facility. These are not part of the basket and are unaffected by any basket choices.
- **The flexible basket** — a personal pool the employee steers, choosing benefits from a menu and being reimbursed against real spend, up to a ceiling set by their tenure.

The two layers are entirely separate. What follows describes each.

## Part 4 — The flexible basket

### The pool

The pool is the ceiling on what **the company contributes** to an employee's basket in a year. It is set by employment type and tenure band, and grows on an accelerating curve — each band's increase is larger than the one before.

| Tenure band | Full-time pool | Part-time pool |
| :---- | ----: | ----: |
| 6 months – 2 years | 20,000 | 14,000 |
| 2 – 4 years | 30,000 | 21,000 |
| 4 – 7 years | 45,000 | 30,000 |
| 7 – 10 years | 65,000 | 42,000 |

*Full-time steps: \+10,000 / \+15,000 / \+20,000. Part-time mirrors the same shape at roughly two-thirds. All figures in EGP.*

### The menu and coverage rates

Eleven benefits across five categories. The company's coverage rate depends on how much the benefit develops the employee — the expression of Principle 4:

- **Wellbeing essentials — 100% covered** *(zero friction; no employee share)*
- **Developmental — 80% covered** *(a modest employee share anchors commitment)*
- **Lifestyle & convenience — 50% covered** *(supported, not funded)*

| Category | Benefit | Company coverage |
| :---- | :---- | :---: |
| **Health & protection** | Personal medical insurance *(employee + optional dependents)* | 100% |
|  | Annual health check-up | 100% |
| **Wellbeing** | Coaching / therapy | 100% |
|  | Gym membership | 80% |
|  | Sports expenses | 80% |
| **Life & family** | Schooling / education | 80% |
|  | Childcare / nursery | 80% |
|  | Caregiver support | 80% |
| **Personal growth** | Personal learning | 80% |
| **Lifestyle & flexibility** | Mobile device | 50% |
|  | Home-office setup | 50% |

**How coverage draws on the pool.** The coverage rate is the company's share of a benefit's cost; that covered amount is what draws from the pool, and the employee pays the remainder on top.

- *Example — gym at 80%:* a 10,000 membership → the company contributes 8,000 (from the pool), the employee pays 2,000.
- *Example — home office at 50%:* a 12,000 setup → the company contributes 6,000 (from the pool), the employee pays 6,000.
- *Example — annual check-up at 100%:* a 3,000 check-up → the company contributes the full 3,000, the employee pays nothing.

### Medical insurance — the rate card

Medical is a **single benefit** priced from a flat company rate card rather than an amount the employee chooses. It covers the **employee plus any dependents they add** — spouse and children — and is fully covered (100%) and treated as an essential.

| Covered person | Annual premium |
| :---- | ----: |
| Employee, spouse, or any child aged 18+ *(each)* | 8,000 |
| Child under 18 *(each)* | 4,500 |

- The employee is always covered; **dependents are optional**, added in the medical modal and priced per person from the card above. There is no maximum number of children.
- Medical is **exempt from the 50% concentration rule** (see Part 5), because health should face no cap on essential cover. It remains **capped at the pool ceiling** — the basket never funds a premium larger than the pool itself.

## Part 5 — The rules

1. **Choose up to five benefits** from the menu each year **(full-time)**; **part-time employees choose up to two.**
2. **No single benefit may exceed 50% of the pool** *(full-time)*. Combined with the choice limit, this means a full-timer selects between two and five benefits — guaranteeing breadth so the basket always reads as a package. *Medical insurance is exempt from this rule as an essential. Part-time pools are not subject to the 50% cap.*
3. **The pool caps the company's contribution.** Coverage rates (100% / 80% / 50%) determine the company's share of each benefit; the employee funds the remainder, and anything above the pool ceiling.
4. **Reimbursement against proof.** Benefits are paid back against a real, evidenced expense — an invoice, receipt, or proof of payment — up to the company's covered share.
5. **Annual cycle, no carry-over.** Claims are made within the year. Unused pool does not roll over and is not paid out as cash.
6. **The guaranteed core is separate** and is never affected by basket choices.

---

## Part 6 — Full-time vs part-time, at a glance

The two schemes share one philosophy and one menu. They differ in three places:

- **Pool size** — part-time pools are smaller at every band (roughly two-thirds of full-time).
- **Guaranteed core** — part-time employees do not receive the loan facility or the summer allowance.
- **Basket rules** — part-timers choose **up to two** benefits (full-time up to five) and are **exempt from the 50% single-benefit cap**.

Everything else is identical: the same eleven-benefit menu and the same coverage rates. Everyone shops the same shelf; the wallet, the core, and the choice limits simply differ.
